# Glide: from contact sheet to film

[Watch the reference](../../docs/assets/showcase/timeline-to-film/glide.mp4).
This is a reading of the finished cut, not a recipe for recreating its colors,
coordinates or exact timings.

**The idea.** At first the viewer is looking at a sequence of shots. When the
playhead reaches one of them, the viewer is looking _through_ that shot. The
key to the transition is already visible in the opening image: one subject
with a recognizable silhouette and movement.

| Moment in Glide   | What to look for                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Opening to 2.63 s | Three moving images read as one edit. The ruler and playhead make their relationship clear before anything grows.                                                                         |
| 2.63–4.20 s       | Follow Lower Falls. The water keeps moving while the neighboring frames, gutters, and type leave together. The main image is never replaced by a second copy that starts from frame zero. |
| 4.20–6.27 s       | Let the shot play. A short period without copy gives the viewer time to look at the picture.                                                                                              |
| 6.27–8.00 s       | The signature appears on the still-moving footage and holds long enough to read.                                                                                                          |

For a new subject, choose the hero by its motion at _both_ sizes. Find a
useful in-point in each source; a shot beginning on black could leave an
apparently empty panel. Let the footage suggest the lettering, spacing,
color, sound and degree of restraint. Avoid implying dates, measurements or
relationships the source material does not establish.

For review, watch the transition once at normal speed before inspecting
frames. Can you point to the same moving detail before, during and after the
push? If it disappears, restarts, or is briefly concealed by a title, that
moment is more useful feedback than a generic request for “smoother motion.”
